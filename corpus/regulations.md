# Greenfield University — General Academic Regulations

*Effective Date: August 1, 2025*
*Approved by the Academic Council, Resolution AC-2025-014*
*Supersedes all previous editions*

---

## 1. Definitions and Scope

### 1.1 Applicability

These regulations apply to all students enrolled in undergraduate and postgraduate programs at Greenfield University, including exchange students and visiting scholars. Part-time students are subject to the same academic standards unless explicitly stated otherwise.

### 1.2 Terminology

- **Academic Year**: Two semesters (Fall and Spring), each consisting of sixteen (16) instructional weeks plus one (1) examination week.
- **Credit Hour**: One hour of classroom instruction per week over a semester, or equivalent laboratory/practical work.
- **GPA**: Grade Point Average on a 10-point scale.
- **SGPA**: Semester Grade Point Average.
- **CGPA**: Cumulative Grade Point Average across all completed semesters.
- **Academic Committee**: The standing committee responsible for academic policy interpretation and student appeals, chaired by the Dean of Academics.
- **Competent Authority**: The Vice-Chancellor or any officer delegated authority under the university statutes.

### 1.3 Academic Calendar

The Registrar's Office shall publish the Academic Calendar at least sixty (60) days before the commencement of each semester. The calendar shall include dates for registration, instruction, examinations, grade submission, and convocation.

## 2. Registration

### 2.1 Course Registration

All students must complete course registration through the University Portal during the designated registration window. Late registration is permitted up to one (1) week after the start of instruction, subject to a late registration fee of ₹2,000.

### 2.2 Minimum and Maximum Course Load

- **Undergraduate**: Minimum 15 credits per semester; maximum 24 credits per semester.
- **Postgraduate**: Minimum 12 credits per semester; maximum 20 credits per semester.
- Students with a CGPA of 8.5 or above may request an overload of up to 4 additional credits with written approval from the Head of Department.

### 2.3 Course Add/Drop

Students may add or drop courses within the first two (2) weeks of instruction without penalty. After the second week and before the eighth week, a dropped course will appear on the transcript with a "W" (Withdrawn) grade. After the eighth week, withdrawal is not permitted except under exceptional circumstances approved by the Academic Committee.

### 2.4 Continuous Registration

Students must maintain continuous registration for each semester. A break in registration exceeding two (2) consecutive semesters without approved leave of absence shall result in automatic termination of enrollment.

---

## 3. Attendance Requirements

### 3.1 Minimum Attendance

Students are required to maintain a minimum of **75% attendance** in each course. Attendance is calculated based on the total number of scheduled classes (lectures, tutorials, and laboratories) for each course independently.

### 3.2 Attendance Tracking

Faculty members shall record attendance at the beginning of each class session. Attendance records shall be uploaded to the University Portal within forty-eight (48) hours of each session. Students may view their attendance status through the portal at any time.

### 3.3 Consequences of Insufficient Attendance

A student whose attendance falls below 75% in any course shall be **debarred from appearing in the end-semester examination** for that course. The student must re-register for the course in a subsequent semester.

### 3.4 Attendance and Internal Assessment

Attendance may constitute up to **10%** of the internal assessment marks for a course, at the discretion of the course instructor. The weightage, if any, shall be communicated to students at the beginning of the semester through the course syllabus.

---

## 4. Examinations

### 4.1 Examination Structure

Each course shall have:
- **Continuous Assessment**: Minimum two (2) assessments during the semester, contributing 40% of the final grade.
- **End-Semester Examination**: A comprehensive examination contributing 60% of the final grade.

### 4.2 Eligibility for End-Semester Examination

A student is eligible to appear in the end-semester examination only if:
1. The student has maintained the minimum required attendance (Section 3.1).
2. The student has no outstanding academic integrity violations pending resolution.
3. The student has paid all required fees for the current semester.

### 4.3 Examination Absence

A student who is absent from an end-semester examination without prior approval shall receive an "FA" (Failed due to Absence) grade. This grade is treated equivalent to an "F" grade for GPA calculations.

### 4.4 Medical Absence from Examination

A student who misses an end-semester examination due to a genuine medical emergency may apply for a **supplementary examination** within seven (7) calendar days of the missed examination. The application must include:
- A medical certificate from a registered medical practitioner.
- Supporting documentation from a hospital or clinic.
- A declaration from the student explaining the circumstances.

The Dean of Examinations shall review the application and may grant permission for a supplementary examination to be held within thirty (30) days of the original examination date.

### 4.5 Re-examination Policy

Students who fail an end-semester examination (grade "F" or "FA") may re-register for the course in the next available semester. There is no provision for a standalone re-examination without re-registration, except as provided under Section 4.4.

## 5. Grading System

### 5.1 Grade Scale

| Grade | Points | Description |
|-------|--------|-------------|
| O | 10 | Outstanding |
| A+ | 9 | Excellent |
| A | 8 | Very Good |
| B+ | 7 | Good |
| B | 6 | Above Average |
| C | 5 | Average |
| P | 4 | Pass |
| F | 0 | Fail |
| FA | 0 | Failed (Absence) |
| W | - | Withdrawn |
| I | - | Incomplete |

### 5.2 Incomplete Grade

An "I" (Incomplete) grade may be awarded when a student has completed at least 70% of the coursework but is unable to complete the remainder due to documented exceptional circumstances. The student must complete the remaining work within six (6) weeks of the following semester, failing which the "I" grade converts to an "F" grade.

### 5.3 Grade Appeals

A student may appeal a grade within fifteen (15) calendar days of grade publication. The appeal must be submitted in writing to the Head of Department, who shall constitute a review committee of three (3) faculty members. The committee's decision shall be final and communicated within twenty (20) working days.

## 6. Academic Standing

### 6.1 Good Standing

A student is in good academic standing when their CGPA is **5.0 or above** and they have no pending disciplinary actions.

### 6.2 Academic Probation

A student whose SGPA falls below **5.0** in any semester shall be placed on **academic probation**. During probation:
- The student's maximum course load is reduced to the minimum required credits.
- The student must meet with their academic advisor at least once per month.
- The student is ineligible for university-funded scholarships and awards.
- The student may not hold elected positions in student organizations.

### 6.3 Removal from Probation

A student on academic probation who achieves an SGPA of 5.0 or above in the subsequent semester shall be removed from probation and restored to good standing.

### 6.4 Academic Dismissal

A student who remains on academic probation for **three (3) consecutive semesters** shall be subject to academic dismissal. The Academic Committee shall review each case individually and may:
- Recommend dismissal.
- Grant one additional probationary semester with specific conditions.
- Recommend transfer to a different program.

## 7. Academic Integrity

### 7.1 Policy Statement

Greenfield University is committed to the highest standards of academic integrity. All forms of academic dishonesty, including plagiarism, cheating, fabrication, and facilitating dishonesty, are strictly prohibited.

### 7.2 Penalties for Academic Dishonesty

First offense:
- Zero marks in the assessment in question.
- Written warning placed in the student's file.
- Mandatory completion of an academic integrity workshop.

Second offense:
- "F" grade in the course.
- One-semester suspension.
- Notation on the transcript.

Third offense:
- Permanent expulsion from the university.
- Notation on all academic records.

### 7.3 Investigation Procedure

Upon receiving a report of suspected academic dishonesty, the course instructor shall report the matter to the Disciplinary Committee within five (5) working days. The committee shall provide the student an opportunity to respond within ten (10) working days before making a determination.

## 8. Academic Leave and Withdrawal

### 8.1 Leave of Absence

A student may apply for a leave of absence for up to two (2) consecutive semesters. The application must be submitted at least thirty (30) days before the start of the semester. The Dean of Student Affairs shall approve or deny the application within fifteen (15) working days.

### 8.2 Grounds for Leave

Approved grounds include:
- Medical reasons (with supporting documentation).
- Family emergency.
- Military service.
- Approved internship or co-op programs exceeding one semester.

### 8.3 Withdrawal from the University

A student who wishes to permanently withdraw from the university must submit a formal withdrawal application to the Registrar's Office. The effective date of withdrawal shall be the date the application is received. Refund of fees shall be governed by the Fee Schedule (see fee_schedule.md).

### 8.4 Readmission After Withdrawal

A student who has withdrawn may apply for readmission within three (3) years of the withdrawal date. Readmission is not guaranteed and is subject to:
- Availability of seats in the program.
- Review by the Academic Committee.
- Completion of any pending academic or financial obligations.
- Students readmitted after more than two (2) years must follow the curriculum in effect at the time of readmission.

## 9. Academic Probation and Recovery

### 9.1 Recovery Plan

Upon being placed on academic probation, the student's academic advisor shall develop a written recovery plan within two (2) weeks. The plan shall include:
- Identification of weak areas.
- Recommended courses for the next semester.
- Required use of academic support services (tutoring, writing center, etc.).
- Monthly progress meetings with the advisor.

### 9.2 Course Repetition

A student may repeat a course in which they received an "F", "FA", or "D" grade. Both the original and repeated grades appear on the transcript, but only the higher grade is counted in the CGPA calculation. A course may be repeated at most two (2) times.

### 9.3 Maximum Duration

The maximum duration for completion of a degree program is:
- **Undergraduate**: Eight (8) years from the date of initial enrollment.
- **Postgraduate**: Five (5) years from the date of initial enrollment.
- Extensions beyond the maximum duration may be granted by the Academic Committee in exceptional circumstances, limited to one (1) additional year.

## 10. Transfer and Credit Recognition

### 10.1 Transfer Credits

Students transferring from other recognized universities may receive credit for equivalent courses completed with a grade of "B" or above. A maximum of **40%** of the total credits required for the degree may be transferred.

### 10.2 Credit Recognition Procedure

Transfer credit applications must be submitted within the first semester of enrollment at Greenfield University. The relevant department shall evaluate course equivalency based on syllabus comparison, credit hours, and assessment methods. The department's recommendation shall be forwarded to the Academic Committee for final approval.

## 11. Committees and Authority

### 11.1 Academic Committee

The Academic Committee consists of:
- Dean of Academics (Chair)
- Two Heads of Department (rotating annually)
- Two faculty members elected by the Academic Council
- One student representative (elected by the Student Council)

The committee meets at least once per month during the academic session.

### 11.2 Powers of the Academic Committee

The Academic Committee has the authority to:
- Interpret these regulations in specific cases.
- Grant exceptions to academic regulations in documented exceptional circumstances.
- Hear and decide academic appeals.
- Recommend policy changes to the Academic Council.
- **Waive any academic requirement** when the committee determines that strict application would result in manifest injustice, provided that a two-thirds majority of the committee members present approve the waiver.

### 11.3 Disciplinary Committee

The Disciplinary Committee is a separate body responsible for:
- Investigating allegations of misconduct.
- Conducting hearings.
- Recommending penalties.
- Its decisions may be appealed to the Vice-Chancellor within thirty (30) days.

## 12. Appeals

### 12.1 Right to Appeal

Every student has the right to appeal any academic decision that materially affects their academic standing, progression, or enrollment. Appeals must be made in writing.

### 12.2 Appeal Process

1. **First Level**: Appeal to the Head of Department within fifteen (15) calendar days of the decision.
2. **Second Level**: If unsatisfied, appeal to the Academic Committee within fifteen (15) calendar days of the departmental decision.
3. **Final Level**: Appeal to the Vice-Chancellor within thirty (30) calendar days of the Academic Committee's decision. The Vice-Chancellor's decision is final.

### 12.3 Appeal Grounds

Valid grounds for appeal include:
- Procedural irregularity.
- New evidence not available at the time of the original decision.
- Disproportionate penalty.
- Bias or conflict of interest.

### 12.4 Interim Measures

During the pendency of an appeal, the student's enrollment status shall not be altered unless there are safety concerns. The student may continue attending classes pending the outcome of the appeal.

## 13. Exceptional Circumstances

### 13.1 Definition

Exceptional circumstances are defined as events beyond the student's control that significantly impair the student's ability to meet academic requirements. Examples include but are not limited to:
- Serious illness or hospitalization.
- Bereavement of an immediate family member.
- Natural disaster affecting the student's residence.
- Documented mental health crisis.

### 13.2 Documentation Requirements

Claims of exceptional circumstances must be supported by:
- Relevant official documentation (medical certificates, death certificates, police reports, etc.).
- A personal statement from the student.
- Where applicable, a supporting statement from a university counselor or advisor.

### 13.3 Remedial Measures

Upon verification of exceptional circumstances, the Academic Committee may grant one or more of the following:
- Extension of assignment deadlines.
- Permission to take supplementary examinations.
- Retroactive withdrawal from courses without academic penalty.
- Adjustment of attendance requirements for the affected period.
- Temporary reduction in course load below the minimum requirement.
